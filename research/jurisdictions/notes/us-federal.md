# us-federal — United States federal layer — researched 2026-10-07

**Pattern:** SPECIAL_AREAS (refuge units) + OTHER (flyways and duck/dove zones are state/county lists, zones are *state-established*), with HUNTER_CLASS_SPECIFIC (Duck Stamp, 16+). **Strategy:** JURISDICTION_WIDE / NON_POLYGON_RESOLVER for flyways (state and county list lookup) and the Duck Stamp rule; AUTHORITY_GEOMETRY (USFWS service) for refuge boundaries; migratory-bird zones/seasons are NOT federal geography — they are state geography under federal authorization conditions.

## Controlling instruments (eCFR as of 2026-10-01, HTTP 200 via versioner API, fetched 2026-10-07)
- 50 CFR 20.107 Definitions (Subpart K, "Process for Issuing Authorization Conditions for Migratory Game Bird Hunting"). Flyways quoted in full in the row. Key: "Zone means a geographic contiguous boundary for which an independent hunting season ... may be established ... Zones are established by States and approved by the Service." and "Area means a federally established geographic boundary within which migratory game bird hunting may occur."
- 50 CFR 20.100, 20.115 (duck zone/split configurations: one, two, three or four zones), 20.119 (bracket: "Not start prior to September 1; Not end after March 10, except ... ducks, mergansers, and coots may not end later than January 31; Not exceed 107 days"), 20.121 (seasons communicated by the "Migratory Game Bird Hunting Memorandum" with a notice of availability in the Federal Register and posted on the Service website), 20.110 (tribal reservations), 20.108 (nontoxic shot zones: all 50 states), 20.132 (Alaska subsistence).
- **Currency flag:** the Federal Register shows Rule 2026-17733 (2026-08-31) and a direct final rule 2026-12951 / proposed rule 2026-12955 (2026-06-26) "changing the administrative process for authorizing seasonal migratory game bird hunting"; the eCFR Subpart K I fetched already reflects the new "authorization conditions / memorandum" process. Season dates are therefore no longer federal CFR tables; they are set by states within the Memorandum. The Memorandum itself was not located/read. Do not assume the old 50 CFR 20.101-20.105 state-by-state frameworks apply.
- 50 CFR 32.2: "Each person 16 years of age and older shall secure and possess a Migratory Bird Hunting Stamp while hunting migratory waterfowl." (Duck Stamp requirement; also applies on refuges.) The statute 16 USC 718a page fetched did not yield text; not quoted. FWS Duck Stamp URL guess returned 404.
- 50 CFR 32.1-32.8, 32.20-32.70: refuge hunting. 32.7 lists refuge units open (e.g. "(uu) Washington. (1) Billy Frank Jr. Nisqually National Wildlife Refuge. (2) Columbia ..."), 32.66 Washington etc. 32.3: "Refuge-specific hunting regulations will not liberalize existing State laws or regulations." 32.2(d): "Each person shall comply with the applicable provisions of the laws and regulations of the State wherein any area is located unless further restricted by Federal law or regulation." 32.2(f): "Regulations, special conditions, and maps of the hunting areas for a particular wildlife refuge are available at that area's headquarters." 32.8 lists areas closed to hunting by proclamation/order.

## Composition with state/provincial layers
- Federal layer = *conditions on top of the state rule*; the state geography decides the open zone. Flyway membership is a function of the state (or specific counties for Colorado, Montana, New Mexico, Wyoming).
- Textual vs polygon: flyways = textual (state names; counties for Montana: "Counties of Blaine, Carbon, Fergus, Judith Basin, Stillwater, Sweetgrass, Wheatland, and all counties east thereof"; Continental Divide lines for CO, NM, WY); duck/dove zones = state-defined; refuges = unit names in CFR with boundary polygons in the FWS service (the CFR says "designated areas of the refuge" and maps at refuge HQ — the *hunt area within a refuge* is generally map/refuge-leaflet defined, not in the CFR text).
- Overlay logic needed: refuge polygon -> refuge-specific rule from 32.20-32.70 + state rule + Duck Stamp. Refuge presence closes or narrows (never widens) state rules (32.3(c)).

## Findings
1. The big finding for the implementation lane: federal geography is a thin layer — flyways are state lists; zones are state-created. The only federal polygons that matter are refuges (and Indian reservations under 20.110).
2. Regulatory process changed in 2026 (see currency flag) — re-verify anything citing the old frameworks.
3. 32.7's list (names) must be joined to the polygon layer's ORGNAME; the layer is wider than the hunt-open list.
