# Global expansion candidates

Planning research only. No country below is supported by North Ground.

| Priority | Country | Structure | Primary authority/source lead | GIS/data accessibility | Languages | Main challenge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | New Zealand | National regulation with regional season notices and game-bird councils | Department of Conservation; Fish & Game New Zealand | Strong public geospatial ecosystem; hunting-permit area layers require review | English, Māori | Different authority by game-mammal vs game-bird context |
| 2 | United Kingdom | Devolved nations plus species-specific statutes and landowner rights | GOV.UK, NatureScot, Natural Resources Wales, DAERA | Strong national open-data portals | English, Welsh | No North American-style universal season/unit model; devolved law |
| 3 | Ireland | National wildlife legislation and ministerial open-season orders | National Parks and Wildlife Service | National open data available; hunting layers unclear | English, Irish | Annual orders and land-permission context |
| 4 | Finland | National hunting law with licence/area services | Finnish Wildlife Agency | Strong digital services; API terms require review | Finnish, Swedish, English | Licence allocations and local game-management associations |
| 5 | Sweden | National law with county/local administration | Swedish Environmental Protection Agency | Strong open data; hunting-area datasets require review | Swedish, English | Landowner hunting rights and county variation |
| 6 | Norway | National legislation with species/area quotas | Norwegian Environment Agency | Strong government data ecosystem | Norwegian, Sami, English | Quota/local land administration and Sami rights |
| 7 | Germany | Federal law plus 16 Länder implementation | Federal Ministry plus Länder hunting authorities | Strong geodata but highly decentralized | German | State-level rules and land/lease system |
| 8 | South Africa | National conservation overlay plus provincial hunting rules | National/provincial environmental authorities | Mixed; province-specific accessibility | English plus provincial official languages | Province variation permits game-farm context and species quotas |
| 9 | Namibia | National wildlife and permit regime with conservancy/private-land context | Ministry of Environment Forestry and Tourism | Moderate; official digital source depth uncertain | English | Permit/concession and community conservancy frameworks |
| 10 | Australia | State/territory regulation; game hunting exists only in specific jurisdictions | State agencies such as NSW DPI and Game Management Authority Victoria | Strong state portals | English | Several states prohibit or narrowly limit recreational hunting; no national huntability assumption |

Recommended order after North American claim-level coverage: New Zealand, United Kingdom/Ireland, then Nordic countries. Germany is data-rich but localization and federalism increase maintenance cost. Southern African candidates require careful separation of wildlife law, permit systems, private/conservancy land, and international trade rules.
